# MSSN Remo ICT Quiz 2026 — Rules & Regulations

**Event:** MSS Week 2026 ICT Quiz  
**Organiser:** MSSN Remo Area Council  
**Theme:** *“Anchored in Deen, Illuminated in Dunya and Digital.”*  
**Focus:** *“The Muslim Student in the Digital Age: Knowledge, Skills and Responsibility.”*  
**Date:** Thursday, 26th November 2026  
**Platform:** MSSN Remo ICT Quiz Arena (web app + candidate tablets + projector display)

> These rules describe the standard three-round competition. The organiser may enable only one or two rounds for a particular event. In that case, the rules for the enabled rounds apply. Official question counts, timers, qualification numbers and scoring values are announced before each round and may be adjusted in the admin settings.

---

## 1. Purpose and spirit of the competition

The ICT Quiz exists to:

1. Test understanding of the official MSS Week 2026 ICT syllabus.
2. Reward accurate knowledge, practical skill and responsible digital judgement.
3. Give students from every school — including schools with limited ICT facilities — a fair opportunity to compete.
4. Promote honesty, respect, discipline and Islamic adab in the use of technology.

Every participant is expected to compete with **knowledge, skill and responsibility**.

---

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Candidate** | A registered secondary school student competing in the quiz. |
| **School coordinator / teacher** | The staff member responsible for registering and supervising candidates from a school. |
| **Admin** | The official controlling rounds, timers, questions, qualification and event settings. |
| **Scorekeeper** | An official authorised to monitor scores and make documented score adjustments. |
| **CBT** | Computer-Based Test used for Round 1. |
| **Display / projector screen** | The public screen showing questions, countdowns, reveals and standings. |
| **Candidate code** | The unique six-character access code issued to one candidate. |
| **Server** | The official quiz application. Server time and server decisions are final. |
| **Question group** | A named bank of questions assigned to a round. |
| **Reveal** | The moment the correct answer, selected answer and points are shown. |

---

## 3. Eligibility and registration

1. A candidate must be a **secondary school student** and must be registered by an authorised school coordinator or the organiser.
2. Each candidate receives **one unique candidate code**. Codes are not transferable.
3. A candidate may compete only under the name and school submitted at registration.
4. Substituting another student, impersonating a candidate, or competing with another candidate’s code is prohibited.
5. The organiser may require a student ID, school letter or other verification before the quiz.
6. Candidates must be present at the venue before the announced registration cut-off.
7. Late arrival does **not** automatically create a new attempt or extra time. The organiser may allow a late candidate to join an open CBT window, but the global round deadline still applies.

---

## 4. Devices, access and connectivity

1. Each candidate should use **one tablet or phone** with a modern browser: Chrome, Safari, Edge or Firefox.
2. One candidate code should be active on **one device at a time**. Sharing a code, using multiple devices, or allowing another person to answer is prohibited.
3. Candidates must join at the official URL using their six-character code.
4. The candidate code must be kept private. Do not display it publicly or share it with another candidate.
5. Candidates must keep the quiz tab open and the device charged. Screen locking, sleeping, excessive notifications or switching apps during a timed question may cause missed answers.
6. If a device disconnects, the candidate should reload the page and sign in again with the same code. The server will restore the attempt where possible.
7. Refreshing the page does **not** stop the timer. The server deadline continues.
8. The organiser may require tablets to be in flight mode, or connected only to the official quiz network, to reduce distraction.
9. The use of another device, phone, smart watch, textbook, note, external help, messaging app, search engine or AI assistant during a timed round is prohibited unless explicitly allowed by the organiser.
10. The projector display and sound system are controlled by the host. Candidates must not interfere with the display computer, network equipment or speaker system.

---

## 5. Competition structure

The standard competition has three independently switchable rounds:

| Round | Name | Format | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Round 1** | CBT elimination | Individual multiple-choice test | 50 questions · 45 minutes · 2 points per correct |
| **Round 2** | Pick a number | Candidate chooses an unused number; timed question; projector reveal | 22 numbers · 60 seconds · 50 base + 25 speed bonus |
| **Round 3** | First to answer | Race: first submitted answer locks the question | 22 questions · 30 seconds · 50 base + 25 speed · −5 wrong |
| **Qualification** | Top candidates | Highest Round 1 scores advance | Top 8 by default |

The organiser may:

- run **CBT only**;
- run **Round 2 or Round 3 only**, using all active candidates;
- run any combination of rounds;
- change question counts, timers, groups and points before a round starts;
- conclude a round or the entire event at any time.

Once a round begins, the settings in force for that round apply. Later changes apply only to the next round or question unless the organiser states otherwise.

---

## 6. Round 1 — CBT elimination

### 6.1 Starting

1. The admin opens Round 1.
2. Each candidate signs in and presses **Start test**.
3. The test may reshuffle question order for each candidate. Two candidates may therefore see the same questions in different orders.
4. The number of CBT questions and the duration are announced before the round. The default is **50 questions in 45 minutes**.
5. The timer is controlled by the server, not the candidate’s device clock.

### 6.2 Answering

1. Candidates may move forward and backward between questions.
2. Answers are saved automatically when selected.
3. A candidate may change an answer before final submission.
4. Each question has one correct option. A correct answer earns the configured CBT points; a wrong answer earns zero. There is no negative marking in Round 1 by default.
5. Candidates may submit early. Once submitted, answers cannot be changed.
6. When the global CBT time expires, all active attempts are submitted automatically at their current answers.
7. Late entry does not extend the global CBT deadline. A candidate who starts late has less time.

### 6.3 Completion

1. A submitted candidate sees a confirmation screen with their current score and correct count.
2. A candidate whose code has already submitted cannot retake the CBT unless the admin resets that candidate’s CBT attempt or opens a fresh CBT.
3. When the admin ends the CBT, the server finalises every active attempt.
4. Starting a fresh CBT clears previous CBT sessions and answers. Previous CBT attempts are void for the new round.

### 6.4 Fairness rules

- Do not share questions, answers, screenshots or question order with other candidates.
- Do not use AI, search engines, messaging apps, notes or another person to answer.
- Do not attempt to open developer tools, modify the page, intercept network traffic or attack the server.
- A candidate found cheating may have the attempt voided and may be disqualified from the competition.

---

## 7. Qualification for the live rounds

1. After the CBT is closed, the admin ranks candidates by total score.
2. The top **N** candidates qualify, where N is announced before the event (default: **8**).
3. If fewer than N candidates are registered, all eligible candidates may qualify.
4. The admin may manually adjust qualification in exceptional circumstances, including verified technical failure. The reason should be recorded.
5. A candidate who does not qualify may remain in the hall as a spectator but may not answer live questions.
6. If the event runs Round 2 or Round 3 without Round 1, the organiser may allow all active candidates to participate. This must be announced before the round.

### Tie-breaking

If candidates tie on score, ranking is determined by:

1. Higher total score;
2. Higher Round 3 score (if played);
3. Higher Round 2 score (if played);
4. Alphabetical order of candidate name.

The admin’s published qualification list is final unless a verified scoring error is corrected.

---

## 8. Round 2 — Pick a number

### 8.1 Turn order

1. Qualified candidates are placed in a turn order. The order may be based on CBT ranking or may be shuffled by the admin.
2. Only the **active candidate** may pick a number and answer.
3. The active candidate’s name and school are shown on the projector.
4. If the active candidate is absent, unable to continue or declines, the admin may skip the candidate or end the round.

### 8.2 Picking a number

1. The number board contains only unused numbers.
2. The active candidate chooses **one unused number** on their tablet.
3. Once picked, the number is removed from the board and cannot be picked again.
4. After the pick, a **5-second countdown** (configurable) runs on the tablet and projector.
5. The question is then revealed on the active tablet and the projector.

### 8.3 Answering

1. The active candidate has the configured answer time — default **60 seconds**.
2. The candidate selects an option. On most screens they must confirm with **Lock answer**.
3. Once the answer is locked, it cannot be changed.
4. A correct answer earns:

   **Base points + speed bonus** proportional to the time remaining.

   Default: **50 base + up to 25 speed bonus**.
5. A wrong answer or timeout earns **0 points**. There is no negative marking in Round 2 by default.
6. When the answer is submitted or time expires, the selected answer, correct answer, points, candidate name and school are revealed.

### 8.4 Rotation

1. After a reveal, the admin presses **Next** to give the next candidate the privilege of picking an unused number.
2. Rotation continues until:
   - all numbers are picked;
   - the admin ends the round; or
   - no eligible candidates remain.
3. Candidates may receive more than one turn if numbers remain after the rotation.
4. The admin may end Round 2 at any time. Scores already earned remain.

---

## 9. Round 3 — First to answer

### 9.1 Format

1. All eligible finalists see the same question and four options.
2. The first answer received by the **server** locks the question.
3. Once an answer is submitted, no other candidate may answer that question.
4. The first answer locks the question **even if it is wrong**.
5. A candidate has no second chance on the same question.

### 9.2 Timing and scoring

1. The race clock is set by the admin — default **30 seconds**.
2. The server records the response time.
3. A correct answer earns:

   **Base points + speed bonus** based on remaining time.

   Default: **50 base + up to 25 speed bonus**.
4. A wrong first answer earns the configured penalty:

   **Default: −5 points.**

   The penalty is deducted from the candidate’s total. The organiser may set a different penalty before the round, including 0.
5. A timeout with no answer earns **0 points**; it does not incur the wrong-answer penalty.
6. The question is then revealed with the first candidate’s name, school, selected answer, correct answer, response time and points.

### 9.3 Continuous / automatic run

The admin may enable **auto-run** for Round 3:

- When enabled, the next question starts automatically after the configured reveal delay — default **5 seconds**.
- When disabled, the admin starts each next question manually.
- The admin may stop, end or reset Round 3 at any time, even while auto-run is enabled.
- A countdown to the next question may appear on the projector.

### 9.4 Fairness

- Only the first submitted answer counts. Do not press multiple options or attempt multiple devices.
- A wrong answer intentionally submitted to block others still incurs the penalty and may be reviewed as misconduct.
- Deliberately random or irresponsible picking is discouraged by the negative penalty and may be addressed by the organiser if it is clearly intended to disrupt the competition.

## 10. Scoring and leaderboard

### 10.1 Round scores

| Round | Correct answer | Wrong answer | Timeout |
|---|---|---|---|
| Round 1 | 2 points per correct (default) | 0 | 0 |
| Round 2 | 50 base + up to 25 speed (default) | 0 | 0 |
| Round 3 | 50 base + up to 25 speed (default) | −5 (default) | 0 |

Actual values are those announced by the organiser before the round.

### 10.2 Overall total

The overall leaderboard is the sum of:

```
Round 1 score + Round 2 score + Round 3 score + manual adjustments
```

Manual adjustments may be positive or negative and must have a recorded reason. They are visible in the audit log.

### 10.3 Leaderboard order

The leaderboard is sorted by:

1. Total score — highest first;
2. Round 3 score — highest first;
3. Round 2 score — highest first;
4. Candidate name — alphabetical.

### 10.4 Score corrections

A scorekeeper or admin may correct a verified error. The correction must include a reason and is written to the audit log. Corrections made before the next round begins are applied to the final total. The organiser’s final result is binding after the event is concluded.

---

## 11. Timing rules

1. All timers are controlled by the **quiz server**.
2. Device clocks, browser timers and network delay do not override the server.
3. The projector and candidate tablets display the server countdown, but the server decides whether an answer was received before the deadline.
4. A late answer is rejected even if the tablet still shows a small amount of time.
5. Once a round is closed, no further answers are accepted.
6. The admin may end a round before its scheduled end. Scores at that moment stand.

---

## 12. Technical failures and retries

1. A candidate experiencing a technical problem must **immediately raise a hand** and inform a teacher, coordinator or official. Do not wait until the end of the round.
2. The candidate should reload the page and sign in again with the same code. The server will restore the attempt where possible.
3. The timer continues during a disconnection or reload unless the organiser decides otherwise.
4. The admin may:
   - reset one candidate’s CBT attempt;
   - allow a candidate to restart;
   - add time where the platform permits;
   - make a documented score adjustment;
   - allow a replacement device.
5. A technical complaint raised after a round has closed may be refused if it cannot be verified.
6. The organiser may pause or restart a round for a venue-wide failure such as power loss, projector failure or network outage.
7. Decisions on technical failures are made by the organiser and are final.

---

## 13. Conduct and prohibited actions

The following are prohibited:

1. Impersonation or use of another candidate’s code.
2. Sharing answers, questions, screenshots or question order.
3. Using AI, search engines, messaging apps, notes or unauthorised devices during a timed round.
4. Taking photographs or recordings of live questions before the reveal.
5. Attempting to access the admin console, database, server or another candidate’s session.
6. Tampering with the projector, network, router, speaker, tablets or power supply.
7. Deliberately disrupting the quiz, harassing participants, or using abusive language.
8. Submitting multiple answers through multiple devices or tabs.
9. Colluding with another candidate or receiving outside assistance.
10. Any form of cheating, deception or misconduct.

Penalties may include:

- warning;
- voiding the affected answer;
- deduction of points;
- loss of a turn;
- disqualification from a round;
- disqualification from the competition.

The organiser may also refer serious cases to the school or relevant authority.

---

## 14. Deen + Digital code of conduct

All participants, teachers and officials are expected to observe Islamic adab:

1. **Honesty (`sidq`)** — do not cheat, impersonate or misrepresent your work.
2. **Trust (`amanah`)** — protect your code, your device and other people’s data.
3. **Justice (`adl`)** — respect the rules even when a decision does not favour you.
4. **Respect** — no mockery, insults, backbiting or harassment, online or in the hall.
5. **Privacy** — do not photograph, record or share another person without consent.
6. **Verification** — do not spread unverified claims about results, questions or other candidates.
7. **Humility** — winning is not a licence to belittle others; losing is not a licence to accuse others without evidence.
8. **Beneficial knowledge** — use what you learn to serve your school and community.

## 15. Roles and authority

### Admin

The admin may:

- open, close, end or reset rounds;
- set question counts, timers, groups and scoring;
- qualify candidates;
- conclude the event;
- correct verified scoring errors;
- disqualify a candidate for misconduct.

### Scorekeeper

The scorekeeper may:

- view live scores and round progress;
- make documented score adjustments;
- assist the admin with verification.

The scorekeeper may not reset the competition, edit candidates or change event settings unless authorised by the organiser.

### Teachers / school coordinators

Teachers may:

- register and supervise their candidates;
- verify identity;
- communicate with the organiser;
- support a candidate during a verified technical failure.

Teachers may not answer questions for candidates or influence live scoring.

### Candidates

Candidates must:

- follow the rules;
- protect their access code;
- answer only on their own device;
- respect officials and other candidates;
- accept the final decisions of the organiser.

---

## 16. Protests and appeals

1. A protest must be raised **immediately** — before the next question or before the round is closed — through the candidate’s teacher or coordinator.
2. The protest should state:
   - candidate name and code;
   - round and question number;
   - what happened;
   - the remedy requested.
3. The admin and scorekeeper will review the server record, timestamps and any available evidence.
4. Only verified technical or scoring errors will lead to a correction.
5. A protest about the correctness of a question must be based on the official syllabus/source, not personal opinion.
6. The organiser’s decision is final. Repeated or abusive protests may lead to disqualification.

---

## 17. Data, privacy and media

1. Candidate names, schools and scores may be displayed on the projector and published in official results.
2. Candidate access codes are private and should not be published.
3. Personal data is used only for registration, competition administration and results.
4. Official photographs or recordings may be taken only by authorised persons. Individual consent should be obtained where required.
5. Candidates and teachers must not record or publish another person’s image, voice or private information without consent.
6. The organiser may retain results and audit records for verification and future planning.

---

## 18. Results, prizes and finality

1. The final leaderboard is the official result after the event is concluded.
2. Prizes are awarded according to the organiser’s published categories.
3. A candidate must be present to receive a prize unless the organiser approves an alternative.
4. The organiser may withhold or withdraw a prize if misconduct is established.
5. The final results and decisions of the organiser are binding.

---

## 19. Changes to these rules

1. The organiser may change settings, question counts, timers or scoring before a round starts.
2. Any change must be announced clearly to candidates and coordinators.
3. Changes made after a round has started apply only to the next round or question, unless the organiser explicitly restarts the round.
4. These rules are interpreted together with the official event instructions and the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria.
